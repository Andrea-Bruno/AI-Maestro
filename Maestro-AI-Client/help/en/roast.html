Roast Monitor — The Heart of the Machine

The Roast panel is where you actually roast coffee. It shows you everything in real time: the temperature inside the drum and inside the beans, how fast the temperature is rising, and all the important moments like when the beans crack. You can start a roast, watch it on the chart, add notes as you go, and save the whole thing as a profile when you are done.

How to do a roast — step by step

1 Press Start Roast. The timer starts and the chart begins recording. Load your coffee into the machine — this moment is called the CHARGE.

2 Watch the two temperature lines on the chart. The blue line (BT, Bean Temperature) shows how hot the beans are. The orange line (ET, Environment Temperature) shows the air temperature inside the drum. The orange line should always stay above the blue one.

3 Keep an eye on the RoR (Rate of Rise) — the green dashed line. It tells you how fast the beans are heating up. A good roast has a RoR that gradually goes down. If it starts going up, the beans may get scorched.

4 When the beans reach the color and development you want, press ⏹ DROP. This marks the moment you take the beans out of the drum. The chart keeps running to show the cooling curve.

5 When you are happy and the cooling is done, press ✓ END. The roast is saved as a profile automatically.

DROP marks the moment on the chart, END closes and saves the session. You can do other things (like cooling) between DROP and END.

The Chart — what all those lines mean

LineColorWhat it shows
BT (Bean Temperature)solid blueHow hot the coffee beans are right now. Starts low when you load the beans, climbs up during the roast, and should keep climbing steadily. If it flattens, the beans are not roasting anymore.
ET (Environment Temperature)solid orangeHow hot the air inside the drum is. It runs above BT — that's the heat that transfers to the beans. If ET drops too low or crosses BT, something is wrong.
RoR (Rate of Rise)dashed greenThe speed at which BT is rising, measured in degrees per minute. This is the most important number: it must keep going down during the whole roast. A rising RoR means the beans are heating too fast.
Target BT (optional)dotted purpleA reference line showing the ideal temperature curve, generated by the AI Profile tool. Compare your actual roast against this target.

Events and markers on the chart

As the roast progresses, important moments appear as vertical lines on the chart. Each one has a label and the temperature at which it happened.

EventColorWhat it means
CHARGEblackYou loaded the coffee. The timer starts here.
TP (Turning Point)grayThe moment when the beans stop cooling down from the charge and start heating up. The BT curve reaches its lowest point and turns upward.
DE (Dry End)amberThe beans have finished drying. They turn from green to yellow. This happens around 160°C.
FCs / FCe (First Crack)dashed redThe beans make a cracking sound as internal moisture turns to steam. This is a key moment — development (flavor creation) begins here. FCs is used to calculate DTR.
SCs / SCe (Second Crack)dashed purpleA second, quieter crack. Indicates a darker roast. Oils start appearing on the bean surface.
DROPsolid redYou took the beans out. This is the thickest line on the chart.

Phase colors on the chart background

The chart background changes color to show which phase of the roast you are in. This helps you see at a glance where you are.

ColorPhaseWhat happens
yellowDryingWater evaporates, beans turn from green to yellow. From CHARGE to Dry End.
greenMaillardBrowning happens, flavors develop. From Dry End to First Crack.
redDevelopmentAcidity reduces, body develops. From First Crack to DROP.
blueCoolingBeans cool down after DROP.

The telemetry panel (right side)

On the right of the chart you see the live numbers. These are the most important measurements during the roast.

NumberWhat it tells you
TimeHow long the roast has been going, in minutes and seconds.
PhaseWhat stage of the roast you are in right now (Drying, Maillard, First Crack, Development, etc.). Shown as a colored badge.
BTThe current temperature of the beans. Big red number.
ETThe current air temperature inside the drum. Big orange number.
RoRHow fast BT is rising right now. Should be going down.
Delta (BT-ET)The gap between bean temperature and air temperature. Normally ET is higher (positive number). If it turns negative, the drum is cold.
DTR %Development Time Ratio — what percentage of the total roast time was spent after First Crack. Specialty coffee target: 20–25%.
Charge TempThe BT measured at the moment you loaded the coffee. Helps you reproduce the same starting conditions next time.

Heater Power and Airflow

Below the telemetry numbers you see two bars. The Heater Power bar shows how much heat the machine is applying (red). The Airflow bar shows the drum ventilation (blue). These are for monitoring — if your machine supports it, you can adjust them from the machine's own controls.

Recording events during the roast

You can mark important moments as the roast happens. This is useful for noting when you adjusted the heat, when you smelled something changing, or any observation you want to save with the profile.

How to record an event:
1. Pick the event from the dropdown list (Turning Point, Dry End, FC Start/End, SC Start, Drop).
2. Press ✓ Mark to record it at the current second.
3. The event appears as a vertical line on the chart.

You can also type a custom label in the text field and press the + Custom label button to add your own event (for example "Added gas" or "Opened door").

Just selecting the event from the dropdown does NOT record it. You must press ✓ Mark.

Taking manual samples

Press + Sample to manually record a temperature data point right now. Normally the system records data automatically every few seconds, but you can add extra points at any time.

Advanced controls (bottom of the panel)

ButtonWhat it does

Extra Channels

If you have additional sensors (for example measuring the gas pressure or the temperature at a different point), you can add them here. Give the channel a name, enter BT/ET values, and press + to add a sample to that channel.

Scale

Connect a digital scale to weigh the coffee before and after the roast. Enter the weight in grams and press Record. The weight loss is automatically calculated and saved with the profile.

Alarm Sets

You can set up to 5 alarm conditions that trigger when temperature, RoR, or time reaches a certain value. For example, you could set an alarm to beep when you reach First Crack, or even make the machine auto-drop. Give the set a name, pick which alarm number to edit, and press + to add it.

AI Profile

If you enter data about your green coffee (density, moisture, Agtron color, flavor goal, development level), Maestro AI can suggest an ideal temperature curve for that specific coffee. Press Create Target and the AI generates a reference curve that appears on your chart as a purple dotted line.

Glossary

The book icon opens a glossary that explains all the technical terms: BT, ET, RoR, DTR, Delta, AUC, FC, SC, and the roast phases. Tap on any term to see what it means.

Tools Tab — Extra Tools for Your Roasting

The Tools tab gives you handy calculators, measuring tools, and references that help you work better.

Calculator

Convert temperatures between Celsius and Fahrenheit, weights between grams/kilograms/pounds, calculate extraction yield (how much of the coffee dissolves in water), and compute density from weight and volume. Useful for checking your brew recipe or converting units for different machines.

NIR Spectrometer

If you have an NIR (near-infrared) sensor, you can record the light spectrum of your coffee. This helps analyze the chemical composition. Enter the sensor readings (wavelengths and intensities) and press Record to save them with the current session.

Crack Detection

If you have an acoustic sensor, you can detect First and Second Crack by sound. Enter the sound amplitude and time, set a detection threshold, and press Detect. The system records when cracks happened, which is more precise than guessing by ear.

Hybrid Heating

For machines that support multiple heat sources (traditional gas, microwave, infrared), you can set the percentage of each. Press Apply to send the mix to the machine.

External Instruments

Connect and control external devices from here. For example, a Variac (variable transformer) lets you adjust the voltage going to your machine. Set the voltage and press Apply.

Physical Tools & References

Agtron Color Chart: Compare your ground coffee against a standard color disk to objectively measure roast degree. #25 is dark, #100+ is light. This is the specialty coffee standard.
Triangulator (Sampler): A metal scoop that slides into the drum to take a bean sample mid-roast without opening the door. Use it to check color and smell in real time.
Roast Log Sheet: A printable form to hand-write batch notes, weather, times, and sensory impressions. Never loses data, even if the power goes out.

Ambient Conditions

Record the current humidity, pressure, and temperature of the roasting room. These environmental factors affect how the roast behaves, especially on humid or cold days.
